Abroad vs India

Fake ad with your photos — EU court (Russmedia) vs India's IT Rules

Vishal Akshintala · 2026-11-23

Law, decoded: say someone posts a fake ad with your photos and your phone number. The site takes it down in an hour. Is that enough?

In Europe, the answer from the EU's top court is no. In Russmedia Digital (Case C-492/23, Grand Chamber, 2 December 2025), the Court of Justice held under the GDPR that an online marketplace must identify ads containing sensitive data before they are published, check whether the advertiser is the person in the ad, and refuse publication if not, unless there is explicit consent or another GDPR exception. It also can't rely on the hosting exemption in the e-Commerce Directive for these GDPR duties.

India works differently. Under Indian law, the closest provisions are in the IT Rules, 2021. Platforms must make "reasonable efforts" not to host content that impersonates someone or invades their privacy (rule 3(1)(b)). Once you complain, rule 3(2)(b) gives the platform two hours, since the February 2026 amendment, to act on content that impersonates you, including morphed images. In my reading, that's a general duty plus a fast clock after a complaint, not the ad-by-ad check the EU court set out.

Removed in an hour. Copied onto other sites.

  • According to the judgment, on 1 August 2018 an unidentified person posted an untrue ad on publi24.ro presenting a woman as offering sexual services, with her photos, used without her consent, and her phone number.
  • The site removed it less than an hour after she asked. Copies stayed up on other websites.

  • The argument the Court rejected for the GDPR duties:
  • As controller, check ads with sensitive data before they go live.
  • My summary of the ruling (C-492/23, GDPR Arts 5(2), 9, 24 to 26 and 32)

Under the GDPR, the marketplace must:

  • before publication, identify ads that contain sensitive data (Art. 9(1));
  • verify whether the advertiser is the person in the ad;
  • if not, refuse publication, unless there is explicit consent or another Art. 9(2) exception;
  • take security measures so such ads aren't copied onto other sites (Art. 32).

India: the closest provisions

What to do first

  • Take screenshots with the link, date and time.
  • Find the platform's grievance officer; their contact details must be published (r.3(2)(a)).
  • Complain under r.3(2)(b): say whether it impersonates you, shows you in sexual content, or uses morphed images of you.
  • Unhappy with the decision, or not resolved in time? Appeal to the Grievance Appellate Committee within 30 days of the grievance officer's reply (r.3A).
