California AG settles with Lyft — opt-out outside the app

Vishal Akshintala · 2026-11-03

Logged in, using the app, and asked to log in again before you could say no to the sale of your data.

That is what California alleged against Lyft. On 29 October 2026, Attorney General Rob Bonta announced a settlement of $1.8m in civil penalties, resolving allegations under the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) and the state's Unfair Competition Law. The office calls it its ninth enforcement action under the CCPA.

What I find useful here is that the alleged problem is not a missing opt-out. A webpage existed. The complaint is about the route to it: a series of links from the in-app privacy policy, a second login, and further personal information, from people Lyft already knew because they had to log in to use the app.

So for product teams, the question goes beyond "do we offer the right?" to "how many steps does it take, and where do they start?" The last slide before the end has four questions to try on your own app.

$1.8m in civil penalties, resolving allegations against Lyft.

  • Laws cited: CCPA, Unfair Competition Law
  • Began with: March 2025 geolocation sweep
  • The office's CCPA actions: This is the ninth

What California alleged:

  • since mid-2023, Lyft sold or shared personal information to target and display ads in its app;
  • Lyft offered no in-app way to opt out;
  • users followed a series of links from the in-app privacy policy to a webpage;
  • there, they had to re-enter their login and submit further personal information.
  • California AG press release, 29 Oct 2026

The right existed. The route was the issue.

  • Businesses "cannot throw up unnecessary roadblocks", the Attorney General said.
  • The CCPA gives consumers the right to ask a business to stop selling or sharing their personal information.
  • California's point: users had to log in to use the app, so Lyft already knew who they were.

What Lyft must do:

  • pay $1.8m in civil penalties;
  • provide an in-app opt-out option;
  • notify California consumers of their right to stop the sale of their personal information.
  • California AG press release, 29 Oct 2026

Four questions to ask

  • Can a signed-in user opt out inside the app?
  • How many steps does it take, starting from the home screen?
  • Do you ask for details you already hold?
  • Does the opt-out cover both selling and sharing, including ad targeting?
