RBI fraud rules from 1 Jan 2027 — who pays, and the five-day clock

Vishal Akshintala · 2026-11-20

Law, decoded: pressured into approving a payment? From 1 January 2027, the RBI counts it as fraud.

The Reserve Bank of India issued new Directions on 24 June 2026 (RBI/2026-27/167). They replace the old rules on limiting customer liability for commercial banks and apply to electronic banking transactions made on or after 1 January 2027.

Three things stand out to me. First, a transfer you approve "under coercion or duress" from a fraudster now counts as a fraudulent transaction, so the scam calls that pressure people into paying are inside the rules. Second, the bank still carries the burden of proving you were at fault (it has since 2017), now for these coerced payments too. Third, timing decides a lot: five calendar days to report a third-party breach for zero liability, and five calendar days to report to the bank and on cybercrime.gov.in or 1930 for the one-time small-loss compensation, which covers frauds in the first year of the new rules.

Slide 6 has the steps, in order.

A call. A threat. A transfer you approve.

  • Say a caller claims you are under "digital arrest" and pressures you into approving a payment. (A scenario, not a real case.)
  • Under the new RBI Directions, a transaction the customer approves under coercion or duress from a third party counts as a fraudulent electronic banking transaction (para 4(15A)).

It depends on whose fault it was:

  • Bank: Bank's negligence (e.g. no mandatory alerts, no 24x7 reporting channel): zero liability, reported or not (76L).
  • Breach: Fault elsewhere in the system, like a payment app, gateway or telecom provider: zero liability if you report within five calendar days (76M).
  • You: Your negligence (e.g. sharing an OTP or PIN): you bear the loss until you report it, minus any small-fraud compensation (76N, 76T).
  • The burden of proving customer liability stays on the bank (para 76K), as under RBI's 2017 circular.

₹25,000 maximum one-time compensation for a small fraud where the customer was negligent.

  • Who: an individual, including a sole proprietor, who is a bona fide victim
  • Loss: gross loss up to ₹50,000
  • Amount: 85% of the net loss or ₹25,000, whichever is less
  • Condition: reported to the bank and on cybercrime.gov.in or 1930 within five calendar days

The clocks in the Directions

In this order

  • Report it to your bank on its 24x7 channel and keep the complaint number it sends you.
  • Complain on cybercrime.gov.in or call 1930.
  • Do both at once, and no later than five calendar days after the transaction.
  • If the bank rejects your claim, it has to tell you why. Ask for the reasons in writing.
  • Applies to commercial banks (not small finance, payments, regional rural or local area banks).
