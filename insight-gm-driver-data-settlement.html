GM $220m multistate settlement — driving data sold on to insurers

Vishal Akshintala · 2026-11-11

How often you drove, when, how hard you braked, whether you sped. 36 US attorneys general say General Motors sold that on.

On 29 October 2026 they announced a $220m settlement with GM over its Smart Driver programme (2016 to 2024). They allege the driving and location data went to data brokers, who sold it to insurers, while GM told drivers it would not share with insurers without express consent. GM does not admit any violation.

The part I would show a product team is how consent was obtained. The attorneys general allege dealership staff accepted the terms for customers, with sales incentives tied to sign-ups, and that the OnStar app told more than 370,000 drivers they were not enrolled when they were.

From 30 November 2026, the settlement requires affirmative express consent before GM collects or discloses driving and location data. The proposed Massachusetts judgment lists what does not count: silence, pre-ticked boxes, general terms of use.

$220m to resolve allegations that GM sold drivers' data without telling them.

  • Programme: Smart Driver, 2016 to 2024
  • Data: trips, start and end times, hard braking, speeding
  • Enrolled in New York: over 700,000 drivers

What the attorneys general alleged:

  • GM sold driving and location data to data brokers, who sold it to insurers;
  • GM told drivers it would not share with insurers without express consent;
  • dealership staff accepted the terms on customers' behalf;
  • the OnStar app told over 370,000 enrolled drivers they were not enrolled.
  • NY and Massachusetts AG releases, 29 Oct 2026

  • Who gives consent:
  • The driver agrees, before driving data is collected or shared.
  • My summary of the settlement's consent terms

What GM must do:

  • get affirmative express consent before collecting or disclosing driving and location data;
  • limit the driving data it collects;
  • run a comprehensive privacy programme, with internal and third-party assessments;
  • oversee and train its dealerships.
  • GM does not admit any violation (proposed consent judgment, Massachusetts)

Consent, defined

  • The proposed Massachusetts judgment excludes silence, pre-ticked boxes, general terms of use, and hovering over or closing content.
  • If a driver does not respond within 10 days of an OnStar enrolment notice, the default is decline.

Four questions to ask

  • Who clicks 'I agree': the user, or someone selling to them?
  • Is consent to share data separate from the terms of use?
  • Does your app show users their real enrolment status?
  • Do partner incentives reward sign-ups to data collection?
