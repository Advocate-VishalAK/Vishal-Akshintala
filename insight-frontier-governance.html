Frontier Governance: Why AI, Privacy, and Space Law Are the Same Problem Wearing Different Clothes

Vishal Akshintala

AI governance, data privacy, and space law read, on the surface, as three separate specialties requiring three separate bodies of expertise. In practice, across every matter this practice has handled in each of them, they turn out to be the same failure pattern recurring in different technical vocabulary: a system built faster than the legal framework meant to govern it, followed by an attempt to retrofit compliance onto architecture that was never designed to support it.

An AI model deployed without a documented risk classification. A consent interface built years before the statute that now governs it existed. A satellite launched into a jurisdictional gap between three legal regimes that don't reference each other. These are not three different problems requiring three different mindsets. They are the same problem — law arriving after the system it needs to govern — appearing in aerospace, in data engineering, in consumer product design.

The frontier is not a place. It is any domain where the system was built before the rule, and where the practitioner's job is retrofitting governance onto architecture that resists it.

What follows from treating these as one problem rather than three is a specific discipline: don't accept a general assurance where a specific, cited standard is available. "AI-compliant" is not an answer; a documented EU AI Act Article 6 classification is. "We take privacy seriously" is not an answer; a purpose-tagged data architecture that an auditor can actually trace is. "Jurisdiction is unclear" is not a stopping point; it's the start of mapping which of several overlapping frameworks actually governs a given question.

This is the actual thesis behind this practice's positioning across litigation and corporate advisory alike: the frontier isn't a specialty. It's a discipline — distrusting general assurance wherever a specific, checkable standard is available instead, whether the matter is an AI model, a data flow, an orbit, or a courtroom.
