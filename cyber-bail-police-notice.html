Police notice, arrest and bail in a cybercrime case

Vishal Akshintala, Advocate

A notice arrives asking you to appear before the cyber police next week. Or the call comes at night: someone in your family has been picked up in an online fraud case. Here is what the law says about each stage.

If you get a notice to appear

Under section 35(3) of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS), when the police decide an arrest is not required, they issue a notice asking the person to appear. The person has a duty to comply with it (section 35(4)).

If you comply, and keep complying, you are not to be arrested for that offence unless the officer records reasons for why an arrest is needed (section 35(5)). If you ignore the notice, the police may arrest you for the offence named in it (section 35(6)).

So the notice is not something to ignore, and not something to walk into unprepared. Read which offence it names, and take advice before you go.

If someone is arrested

The person must be told the grounds of arrest straight away (section 47 BNSS), and a relative or friend they name must be informed of the arrest and where they are held (section 48).

They cannot be kept in police custody for more than twenty-four hours, excluding travel time, without being produced before a Magistrate (section 58).

Is the offence bailable?

It depends on the sections in the FIR. Under section 77B of the Information Technology Act, 2000, offences punishable with three years' imprisonment are bailable. Identity theft (section 66C) and cheating by personation using a computer resource (section 66D) each carry up to three years.

Online fraud cases often add cheating under section 318(4) of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, which carries up to seven years and is listed as non-bailable in the First Schedule of the BNSS. Then bail is for the court to decide.

The three kinds of bail application

Anticipatory bail, before any arrest, from the Court of Session or the High Court (section 482 BNSS). Regular bail from the Magistrate's court (section 480). And bail from the Court of Session or the High Court (section 483).

The sections written in the FIR decide almost everything that follows. Get a copy and read them first.

What to do first

  1. Get the FIR number, the police station and the sections. For a notice, keep the notice itself and the envelope or message it came in.
  2. Do not delete chats, emails or transaction records. They may be the evidence that helps you.
  3. If there has been an arrest, note the time and place, and ask where the person will be produced before the Magistrate.
  4. Speak to an advocate before the date on the notice, or before the first production in court.
