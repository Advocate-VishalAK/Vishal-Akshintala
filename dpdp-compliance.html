Data privacy — Advisory

DPDP Act compliance for companies

Vishal Akshintala, Advocate

Your app collects phone numbers, addresses, maybe Aadhaar details or children's data. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 now has its Rules, and the dates are fixed.

The dates

The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025 and come into force in three stages (rule 1). The Data Protection Board provisions applied at once. Consent Manager registration (rule 4) applied one year later, from 13 November 2026.

The rules that most companies have to act on apply eighteen months after notification, from 13 May 2027: the notice you give people (rule 3), reasonable security safeguards (rule 6), telling people and the Board about a breach (rule 7), verifiable consent for children's data (rule 10), and people's rights over their data (rule 14).

What the Rules ask for

A notice in plain language that says what data you collect and why. Security safeguards you can show, not only describe. A way for people to access, correct and erase their data and withdraw consent.

And a breach process. On becoming aware of a personal data breach, a company must inform the Board without delay and send detailed information within seventy-two hours, and must inform each affected person (rule 7).

The Schedule to the Act sets the penalties. Failing to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a breach can cost up to ₹250 crore.

A privacy policy is the last thing to write. First you need to know what data you hold, why, and where it goes.

How the work runs

  1. A data map and gap assessment: what personal data you hold, why, who it is shared with, and where you fall short of the Act and Rules. You get a written report.
  2. The documents: notices, consent flows, policies, vendor terms and a breach response playbook. A DPIA (a written check of the risks to people before a product or feature goes live) where it is needed.
  3. Implementation alongside your product, engineering and legal teams, so the documents match what the product actually does.
  4. Training for the people who handle the data every day.
