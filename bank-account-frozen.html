Bank account frozen in a cybercrime investigation

Vishal Akshintala, Advocate

You sold something online, or received a payment from someone you barely know. A few days later the bank says there is a hold on your account, on the instruction of the cyber police in another state.

What usually happened

Someone reported an online fraud, on the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal or the 1930 helpline. The money was traced from account to account, and some of it reached yours. The police then asked your bank to stop the money moving.

That does not mean you are accused of anything. Often the account holder is a seller, a trader or a relative who had no idea where the money came from. It does mean the account stays stuck until someone deals with it.

What the law says

A freeze of this kind is usually made under the police power to seize property in section 106 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS). The section covers property suspected to be stolen or found in circumstances that create suspicion of an offence, and it requires the officer to report the seizure to the Magistrate "forthwith".

Attaching property as the proceeds of crime is a different route, under section 107 of the BNSS, and that one needs an application to the Court or Magistrate.

Section 503 of the BNSS lets the Magistrate decide what happens to seized property, including handing it back to the person entitled to it, with or without conditions.

The first question is never "how do I unfreeze it?" It is "who froze it, under which complaint, and for how much?"

What to do first

  1. Ask your bank, in writing, which police unit asked for the hold, the complaint or acknowledgement number, and the amount in dispute.
  2. Collect what shows how that money reached you: the invoice or order, the chat, the delivery proof, your bank statement.
  3. Write to the investigating officer with those documents, and keep a copy of what you send and when.
  4. If the hold stays, an application can be made to the Magistrate who has jurisdiction, asking for the account to be released or for the hold to be limited to the disputed amount. In some cases the High Court is approached instead.

Where I come in

I am an advocate practising in Hyderabad and across Telangana, and I take up matters elsewhere in India on referral. In these cases I find out which unit placed the hold and why, put the representation and documents before the investigating officer, and move the application before the court when that is needed.

No lawyer can promise when or whether a court will lift a freeze. What can be done properly is the paperwork, the explanation of the transaction and the application.
