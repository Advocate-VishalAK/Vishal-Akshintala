AI governance: EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001 and NIST AI RMF

Vishal Akshintala, Advocate

Your product uses AI. A European customer's procurement team sends a questionnaire asking how you classify it under the EU AI Act, and whether you follow ISO/IEC 42001. You have a week to answer.

The EU AI Act dates

The Act applies in stages. Prohibited practices and AI literacy have applied since 2 February 2025, and the obligations for general-purpose AI models since 2 August 2025. The Act became generally applicable on 2 August 2026, with some exceptions.

After the AI Omnibus, which entered into force on 27 July 2026, the rules for high-risk AI in the sensitive uses listed in Annex III apply from 2 December 2027, and for AI in regulated products (Annex I) from 2 August 2028.

For high-risk systems the obligations include risk management, data governance, logging, technical documentation, information for deployers, human oversight, and accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity.

The frameworks customers ask about

ISO/IEC 42001 is the international standard for an AI management system: the policies, roles, risk and impact assessments and reviews an organisation runs around its AI. An organisation can be certified against it.

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework is a voluntary framework from the US National Institute of Standards and Technology, built around four functions: Govern, Map, Measure and Manage.

They are not alternatives to the law. Used well, one governance programme can answer all three.

The first job is classification. Until you know which tier your system falls in, you cannot know which obligations are yours.

How the work runs

  1. An inventory of your AI systems and a classification of each under the EU AI Act, with the reasoning written down.
  2. The assessments: AI impact assessments, and fundamental rights impact assessments where the Act calls for them.
  3. The governance framework: policies, roles, documentation and review cycles, mapped to ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI RMF so one set of work answers several questionnaires.
  4. An audit with a written report where you want an outside view, then implementation alongside your teams, and training for legal, product and engineering.
