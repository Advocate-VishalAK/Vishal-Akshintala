Consent Manager registration: the Board's 17 documents

Vishal Akshintala · 2026-11-16

Want to register as a Consent Manager under India's DPDP Rules? The Data Protection Board has published the list: 17 mandatory documents to have ready before you file.

Rule 4 of the DPDP Rules, 2025 came into force on 13 November 2026, one year after the Rules were notified. Consent Managers are registered entities that run the platform people use to give, manage, review and withdraw their consent. To register with the Board, the applicant needs a net worth of at least ₹2 crore, and the Board's portal now lists what to prepare: governance documents, up to three years of financials, a system architecture document, operational plans and two declarations.

I've grouped all 17 in the slides, in the Board's own order. Start with the ones that depend on other people: changes to your MoA and AoA, the CA certificates, and the architecture document, which has to cover what the Board calls data-blind design.

Straight from the Data Protection Board of India.

  • Every one of them is mandatory.

₹2cr minimum net worth to register as a Consent Manager.

  • The rule: DPDP Rules, 2025, First Schedule, Part A.
  • The proof: A net worth certificate from a practising CA, on their letterhead.

Governance and money

  • Certificate of Incorporation, from the Registrar of Companies.
  • MoA and AoA (documents 2 and 3), both carrying the no-conflict-of-interest obligations (Part B, items 9 and 10), changeable only with the Board's prior approval.
  • Audited financial statements for the last three financial years, or all you have.
  • Turnover certificates for the same years, from a practising CA.
  • Net worth certificate: at least ₹2 crore, from a practising CA.

Platform and people

  • System architecture document: consent lifecycle, data-blind design, security, interoperability, hosting, record-keeping.
  • Statement of operational capacity, signed, on your letterhead.
  • Key managerial personnel and senior management: qualifications, experience, roles.
  • Governance structure and the board's composition, with each director's other directorships.
  • Conflict-of-interest policy covering your dealings with Data Fiduciaries.

Controls and plans

  • Internal audit and compliance monitoring plan: scope, frequency and who is responsible.
  • Business continuity plan: backups, recovery times, and what happens if you stop operating.
  • Business and capability plan: operations, operating model and fees, grievance redressal.
  • Capital structure: share capital and every shareholder above 2%.

Two declarations, then the file rules

  • "Fit and proper" declaration for directors, key managerial personnel and senior management.
  • Declaration that operations will be run in the interests of Data Principals.
  • Every document as a PDF, preferably machine-readable, up to 8 MB each.
